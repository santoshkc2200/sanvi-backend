## Statement surface: 13 distinct statements — why not 50

Each scenario ran `pg_stat_statements_reset()` immediately before its k6 run;
the union across the five shapes is below. **The real query surface a lab can
reach is smaller than 50, and the reason is structural, not methodological:**
three of the five shapes (admin_write, checkout_create, webhook_flood) stop at
the 401/signature boundary — there is no Ory Kratos locally, so authenticated
surfaces contribute almost no SQL (the admin/checkout rows below are the
tenancy resolution, RLS scoping and audit round-trips those requests make
*before* authentication rejects them, plus the checkout's own probe shape).
The storefront and beacon shapes are the only deep journeys, and both are
heavily Redis-cached by design. Padding the table with one-off seed/migration
statements would make it noise. TASK-021's EXPLAIN and statement-count work
(steps 3–4) covers the full surface per use case regardless of what a load
shape touches.

## Pool saturation — captured (the 2026-09-17 gap)

The API and worker now export sqlx pool gauges (`crates/platform/persistence`
`PoolMetrics`, TASK-021 step 0): `db_pool_size_gauge`, `db_pool_idle_gauge`,
`db_pool_acquire_seconds` (histogram) and `db_pool_acquire_failures_total`.
sqlx exposes no acquire-wait hook and read paths check connections out
directly, so `acquire_seconds` is measured by a 1 Hz **probe**: one real
acquire per interval, recording how long it waited. Under saturation the
probe waits behind the queue — that is the number step 5 sizes pools from.

Observed on the API under each shape (60 s; pool `max_connections` at its
shipping default of 10):

| shape | size (max seen) | idle (end) | probe acquires | probe wait sum | failures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| storefront_read (50 rps) | 3 | 3 | 209 | 0.147 s (mean 0.7 ms) | 0 |
| admin_write (20 rps) | 4 | 4 | 291 | 0.202 s (mean 0.7 ms) | 0 |
| checkout_create (10 rps) | 4 | 4 | 355 | 0.242 s (mean 0.7 ms) | 0 |
| webhook_flood (100 rps) | 4 | 4 | 420 | 0.283 s (mean 0.7 ms) | 0 |
| conversion_upload (50 rps) | 4 | 4 | 484 | 0.325 s (mean 0.7 ms) | 0 |

Every probe wait landed in the ≤ 5 ms bucket: at the offered rates the pool
has 6+ idle-capable connections of headroom and no acquire contention. This
section is the before-picture step 5 works from; the same gauges under the
3× run say whether the default of 10 survives.

## What changed since the 2026-09-17 baseline

1. **Release build.** The old capture's release link failed (sqlx_macros
   dylib); this host links. Every latency number from the old capture is a
   debug-build number — steps 2, 3 and 5 take before-numbers from this
   document instead.
2. **Post-erratum seed.** The payments/checkouts row contract is the plan's
   (5,748 payments), so payments-adjacent sizes differ from the old capture.
3. **pg_stat_statements permanent** in compose; no per-capture ALTER SYSTEM.
4. **Pool gauges exist.** The empty "Pool saturation" section of the old
   baseline is filled above.
5. **k6 on Windows.** k6 v2.3.0 (scoop's 0.51 bundle cannot parse the
   harness's optional catch binding). The PERF_MACHINE profile is recorded
   per artifact; this host's 20 logical cores at 16 GB RAM differ from the
   old capture's 10-core/16 GB arm64 MacBook — perf-compare refuses
   cross-profile diffs, so a future baseline tag on *this* machine is the
   comparison base for this task's after-numbers, not the 2026-09-17 one.
